# Sourdough Starter Notes

The starter was created from scratch using equal parts whole wheat flour and filtered water, fed daily for the first two weeks until it reliably doubled in size within 6 hours at room temperature.

Feeding ratio is 1:5:5 by weight — one part starter, five parts flour, five parts water. This ratio keeps the starter mild rather than overly sour, which suits the sandwich loaves we bake most often. A more sour flavor comes from a longer, cooler bulk ferment, not from changing the feeding ratio.

Current feeding schedule is once every 24 hours when kept at room temperature, or once a week if stored in the fridge. When pulling it out of the fridge to bake, it needs two feedings roughly 8 hours apart before it's active enough to leaven a loaf.

The starter is kept in a half-gallon glass jar with a loose-fitting lid — never sealed airtight, since it needs to release gas. A rubber band around the jar marks the starting level after each feeding, which makes it easy to see how much it has risen.

If the starter develops a layer of dark liquid on top (called "hooch"), that means it's hungry and overdue for a feeding. Pour off the hooch and feed as usual; it doesn't need to be thrown away.
